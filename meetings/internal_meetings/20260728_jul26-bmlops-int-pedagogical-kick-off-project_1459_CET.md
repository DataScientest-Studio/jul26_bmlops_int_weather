── Summary ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  This kickoff outlined the MLOps training, covering curriculum, support, validation, project work, and essential learning practices.
  
  ### Key Points
  
  #### MLOps Engineer Role & Skills
  
  The session defined the MLOps engineer role and essential skills for success.
  
  * MLOps engineers design, develop, and deploy models, managing the entire model lifecycle from creation to deployment and retraining.
  * Required skills include machine learning fundamentals (Python, TensorFlow, PyTorch), software engineering (Git, GitHub, clean code, APIs), agile methodology, communication, and cloud computing expertise.
  * The training aims to equip participants with a broad skill set to deliver valuable models to end-users.
  
  #### Pedagogical Support & Resources
  
  Participants learned about available support channels and key personnel for their training journey.
  
  * Kylian serves as the pedagogical cohort leader, ensuring proper training module delivery and material relevance, starting next week.
  * Nicole, the project manager, handles logistical aspects, including links for masterclasses and addressing training-related issues.
  * Support options include a forum for pedagogical questions, teaching assistants for technical debugging (1-hour slots), direct contact via Slack, and email (help@lyra.io) for other issues.
  
  #### Training Structure & Validation
  
  The training structure, including sprints, modules, and validation criteria, was clearly outlined.
  
  * The pedagogical track, a Google Sheet, details all deadlines, modules, and masterclass schedules.
  * The training is divided into five sprints, each with mandatory and optional modules, masterclasses, and project objectives.
  * Validation requires a minimum of 7 out of 9 certifications from mandatory courses, participation in and validation of the project, and attendance at all masterclasses.
  * Each module exam allows three attempts, with feedback provided after each submission; some exams are timed, others require file uploads.
  * Successful validation leads to a Lyra certification and a Sorbonne University certification.
  
  #### Project Work & Evaluation
  
  The project component, from selection to defense, was detailed as a core learning experience.
  
  * Projects simulate real-world ML engineering, requiring participants to design, implement, and test solutions from a business need.
  * Participants can propose their own project topics or select from a catalog, forming groups of 2-4 people based on preferences.
  * Each group receives a dedicated mentor and Slack channel; project access requires validation of Git/GitHub and Linux/Bash modules.
  * Project evaluation focuses on teamwork, communication, best practices, and active participation, culminating in a defense presentation and live demonstration around October 12th.
  
  #### Best Practices & First Objectives
  
  Key recommendations for effective learning and immediate objectives for Sprint 1 were provided.
  
  * Participants are encouraged to communicate actively on Slack, work ahead on modules, and document everything (e.g., using GitHub repositories for notes).
  * Regular group meetings are advised to prepare for mentor sessions and address roadblocks proactively.
  * Sprint 1 objectives, due by August 6th, include completing mandatory modules: Linux and Bash ML, and Docker, which are foundational for subsequent training.
  * Fraud (e.g., delegating work, copying solutions) is strictly prohibited, emphasizing the importance of personal learning and understanding.
